FREQUENTLY EXAMINED QUESTIONS

Does TruthLens AI claim machine learning alone establishes whether news is true?
No. TruthLens AI strictly adheres to the principle that machine learning models cannot replace empirical truth or independent human journalism. The platform produces a model-derived probabilistic credibility index (Likely Genuine, Potentially Misleading, Needs Further Verification) based on linguistic markers, cross-model consensus, and verified factual evidence retrieval.
How does the evidence-based claim verification work?
The system parses verifiable factual assertions (quantities, dates, institutional statements) and queries an index of authenticated records from international wire services (Reuters, AP), peer-reviewed scientific journals (Nature, The Lancet), and certified fact-checkers (PIB, WHO). It strictly enforces zero-hallucination rules and never fabricates citations or URLs.
Does an AI-assisted writing style mean an article is fake news?
No. Our AI-Writing-Style assessment is explicitly labeled as experimental. Many professional newsrooms and journalists utilize AI-assisted drafting and copy editing. High structural uniformity is not proof of misinformation.

TRUTHLENS AI WHITEPAPER

Technical formulation, multilingual NLP design, and ethical credibility protocols.

1. Three-Tier Credibility Formulation vs. Binary Absolutism

TruthLens AI fundamentally rejects binary "True/False" categorization. Binary classification in natural language introduces dangerous false certainty and fails on nuanced reporting, early-breaking stories, and scientific preprints. TruthLens AI computes a continuous 0–100 credibility index mapped into:

  • Likely Genuine (Score 70–100): High empirical grounding, formal tone, verified primary citations.
  • Potentially Misleading (Score 40–69): High sensationalism, emotional panic markers, or uncorroborated assertions.
  • Needs Further Verification (Score < 40 or Disagreement > 50%): Novel claims or sharp model divergence.

2. Multilingual Negation Preservation & NFKC Normalization

Standard NLP stopword removal indiscriminately strips negation tokens (not, never, no, without, Hindi: नहीं, मत), inverting the factual meaning of scientific statements ("Medication did not trigger adverse reactions"). TruthLens AI explicitly shields negation tokens throughout tokenization and lemmatization.

3. Zero-Hallucination Protocol & Primary Repository Matching

Unlike large language models that generate synthetic citations and fabricate non-existent URLs, TruthLens AI extracts candidate assertions and executes exact and semantic distance queries exclusively against an authenticated index of certified repositories (NASA, WHO, Reuters, PIB, Nature). The platform never synthesizes citations.
